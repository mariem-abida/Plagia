Abstract
We use numerics to construct solitary waves in Dirac–Klein–Gordon (in one and
three spatial dimensions) and study the dependence of energy and charge on ω. For the
construction, we use the iterative procedure, starting from solitary waves of nonlinear
Dirac equation, computing the corresponding scalar field, and adjusting the coupling
constant. We also consider the case of massless scalar field, when the iteration pro-
cedure could be compared with the shooting method.
We use the virial identities
to control the error of simulations. We also discuss possible implications from the
obtained results for the spectral stability of solitary waves.

Introduction
While the nonlinear Dirac equation was written down by D. Ivanenko back in 1938 [Iva38]
and the nonlinear Klein–Gordon equation by L. Schiff in 1951 [Sch51a, Sch51b], the rigorous
mathematical study of nonlinear field equations started forming in early sixties, already after
the triumph of Quantum Electrodynamics. The Cauchy problem for the nonlinear wave
equation appears in [Jör61, Seg63]. This research was followed by the study of nonlinear
scattering (stability of zero solution) [Seg66, Str68, MS72], existence of solitary waves [Str77,
BL83, CV86, EGS96], and then linear and orbital stability of solitary waves [Zak67, Kol73,
CL82, GSS87] followed by the ongoing research on asymptotic stability of solitary waves.
The results for systems appear with a delay, particularly so because of the diﬀiculties
caused by nonlocal type of self-interaction. The Dirac–Klein–Gordon system (DKG) – the
focus of the present article – describes fermions interacting with the scalar boson field via
Yukawa coupling. The “Higgs portal” theories suggest that this model may be relevant for
the description of the Dark Matter (see [CKBC25] and the references therein). The relation
between the masses of fermions and bosons (m and M, respectively) in these theories is
presently not clear; one possibility is to have light fermions interacting with the Higgs field,
m ≪M = 125 GeV. On the other side of the spectrum, m ≫M, heavy fermions could
interact via exchange of axions in models based on axial currents ¯ψγ5γµψ or via exchange
of a very light or massless spinless neutral boson called majoron, arising in models with
spontaneous lepton number breaking, based on Yukawa-type coupling terms ¯ψψφ or ¯ψγ5ψφ
[LP07].
DKG, Dirac–Maxwell (DM), and similar systems also admit localized modes (see [EGS96,
Abe98, RN10]), which may or may not have physical relevance in the description of nonper-
turbative effects of quantum field theories; the stability of localized solutions is crucial for
their possible applicability in the description of certain physical phenomena. Remarkably,
the spectral stability of standing waves ϕω(x)e−iωt – that is, the absence of linear instability –
is available in the nonlinear Dirac equation (NLD): see [BC12] for the numeric results on spec-
tral stability in 1D (corroborated numerically and analytically in [CBC+18, Lak18, ARSV23])
and [CMKS+16, BC19b] for numeric and analytic results on spectral stability in higher di-
mensions.
Orbital stability of solitary waves has been shown in a completely integrable
massive Thirring model [PS14, CPS16]. See also the monograph [BC19a] for more details
and references.
The articles [CZ13, CKBC25] suggest spectral stability of some of the solitary waves in
DM and DKG systems. To set up the groundwork for the research in these two systems, it is
convenient to have some basic properties of the corresponding solitary waves. The problem
is that solitary waves in Dirac-based models are not readily available: there are no explicit
formulas known (except for 1D NLD), the proof of their existence is indirect [EGS96], and
the asymptotic behavior is only available in the nonrelativistic limit (see [BC17] for NLD for
ω near m and [CS18] for DM for ω near −m). This is why we have to rely on the numerics
to know the behavior of charge and energy of solitary waves ϕ(x, ω)e−iωt as functions of
frequency, to be able to make at least some predictions regarding stability: the stability of
small amplitude solitary waves is inherited from the nonrelativistic limit of the model (see,
e.g., [CGG14, BC19b]) and then critical points of the charge Q(ω) and zero values of E(ω)
indicate the collision of eigenvalues of the linearization operators at the origin and hence
possibly the border of the stability region [BCS15].
2

In the present article, we give a numerical construction of solitary waves in DKG with
Yukawa coupling. Since one needs to specify two parameters for the construction of DKG
solitary waves, the shooting method is not reliable; instead, we start with the NLD solitary
waves and use the iterative procedure which converges to DKG solitary waves. In the mass-
less case (Dirac equation coupled to the wave equation), both the iterative approach and
the shooting method are available, with the shooting being much more accurate; we show
that indeed both methods are in agreement. The shooting method also allows to construct
solitary waves with nodes (covered by the existence proof from [EGS96]). While we cannot
yet produce accurate results on spectral stability, we will already be able to make some
preliminary conclusions about the location of the stability region based on the dependence
of the energy of ω. We point out that while there are no zero spin massless particles in
the standard model as of now (the only massless particle which is presently known in High
Energy Physics is photon, a spin-1 particle), the conclusion of the present paper is that the
massive spinor field coupled to massless scalar field forms localized states, and we expect
these states to be stable at least for ω near m (“the nonrelativistic limit”).
The DKG system is introduced in Section 2. The numerical setup for the case of massive
scalar field is in Section 3; the massless case is considered in Section 4. The stability prop-
erties are discussed in Section 5. Relevant details about NLD are presented in Appendix A.

is rather large (approximately 0.254m < ω < 0.936m), before nonzero-real-part eigenvalues
start emerging by pairs or by quadruplets from collisions of purely imaginary eigenvalues
which occur for some smaller values of ω.
Since 3D DKG in the limit M/m →∞of heavy scalar field turns into cubic NLD, we
expect a very similar situation with the spectral stability. We expect to have linear instability
for ω near m, followed by the collision at z = 0 of eigenvalues of the linearization operator
at ω0 which corresponds to the minimum of Q(ω) (and also of E(ω) due to (2.14)), followed
by the onset of spectral stability below ω0. We have shown numerically in Section 3 that
the critical value ω0 – the minimum of E(ω) on Figure 3, which corresponds to the upper
boundary of the stability region – increases as M decreases, with ω0 →m as M →0. We
do not observe vanishing of Q′(ω) or E(ω) on (0, ω0), giving us the expectation that the
stability region could again be rather large.

6
Conclusion
We constructed numerically solitary waves in Dirac–Klein–Gordon system in 1D and 3D via
the iteration procedure (starting from solitary waves to the cubic nonlinear Dirac equation,
finding the corresponding boson field, computing the coupling constant to have localized
states in this field, computing the updated boson field and so on). We showed that the
iterative procedure converges rather fast. In the case of massless scalar field in 3D, we were
able to corroborate the results using the one-parameter shooting method (the reduction to
one parameter becomes available when M = 0).
We computed the dependencies of E and Q on ω. Based on these dependencies and
the stability considerations in [BCS15], we expect that Dirac–Klein–Gordon solitary waves
are spectrally stable for ω ≲ω0, with ω0 →0.936m as M →∞(spinor field coupled to
heavy scalar particles) and ω0 →m as M →0 (coupling to very light or massless scalar
particles). The left border of the stability region is presently unknown; we expect that it is
approximately 0.254m in the limit of heavy scalar field, M →∞.
