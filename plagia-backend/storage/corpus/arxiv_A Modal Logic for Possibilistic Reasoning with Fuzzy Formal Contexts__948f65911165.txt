A Modal Logic for Possibilistic Reasoning with
Fuzzy Formal Contexts
Prosenjit Howlader and Churn-Jung Liau⋆
Institute of Information Science, Academia Sinica, Taipei 115, Taiwan
{prosen,liaucj}@iis.sinica.edu.tw
Abstract. We introduce a two-sort weighted modal logic for possibilistic
reasoning with fuzzy formal contexts. The syntax of the logic includes
two types of weighted modal operators corresponding to classical neces-
sity (2) and sufficiency (⊟) modalities and its formulas are interpreted
in fuzzy formal contexts based on possibility theory. We present its ax-
iomatization that is sound with respect to the class of all fuzzy context
models. In addition, both the necessity and sufficiency fragments of the
logic are also individually complete with respect to the class of all fuzzy
context models. We highlight the expressive power of the logic with some
illustrative examples. As a formal context is the basic construct of for-
mal concept analysis (FCA), we generalize three main notions in FCA,
i.e., formal concepts, object oriented concepts, and property oriented con-
cepts, to their corresponding c-cut concepts in fuzzy formal contexts.
Then, we show that our logical language can represent all three of these
generalized notions. Finally, we demonstrate the possibility of extending
our logic to reasoning with multi-relational fuzzy contexts, in which the
Boolean combinations of different fuzzy relations are allowed.
Keywords: weighted modal logic · possibilistic reasoning · formal con-
cept analysis · rough set theory

Introduction
Formal concept analysis (FCA) is a mathematical theory for studying structures
of concepts [32]. In FCA, a formal context (or simply context), aka polarity, is a
triple (G, M, I), where G and M are sets of objects and attributes, respectively,
and I ⊆G×M is a binary incidence relation connecting them. Given a context,
a formal concept is a pair of subsets of objects and attributes (A, B) such that A
is the set of objects possessing all attributes in B and B is the set of attributes
shared by all objects in A. For the formal concept, A and B are called its extent
and intent, respectively. Furthermore, inspired by rough set theory [31], notions
of rough concepts including object and property oriented concepts, have been
also defined [14,33,34].
The theoretical framework of FCA has evolved into an indispensable tool
for knowledge discovery from complex datasets which are usually presented as

formal contexts . Traditionally, most data mining methods represent the discov-

the representation of more complicated knowledge. Moreover, when applying to
decision-making, simple rules can facilitate only shallow reasoning by matching
their antecedents with the decision situation and taking consequents of matched
rules. By employing a logical approach, we can address the expressivity of knowl-
edge representation formalisms for data mining from formal contexts.
For the knowledge representation and reasoning with formal contexts, several
logical formalisms have received much attention [5,9,10,19,20,21,22]. In a recent
work, it is shown that two-sorted Boolean modal logic can serve as a reliable
base for the representation of both formal and rough concepts [23]. The logic is
a synergy of two-sorted modal logic and Boolean modal logic.
On one hand, two-sorted modal logic is an instance of many-sorted polyadic
modal logic, which was studied in [24] and then extended to a many-sorted hy-
brid logic in [25]. Because a formal context has two domains and an incidence
relation, it is an appropriate semantic model for two-sorted modal logic. On the
other hand, Boolean modal logic combines two dual branches of modal logic:
one with the box modality (□) representing necessity, and the other with the
window modality (⊟) representing sufficiency [17]. These two kinds of modali-
ties correspond to basic operators in rough approximations and FCA, and thus
facilitate the representation of different notions of concepts in formal contexts.
While FCA has been an effective tool for data analysis, because of the per-
vasive uncertainty and vagueness of information in complex datasets, the binary
relation in formal contexts may be not two-valued in many applications. In par-
ticular, to deal with vague information in formal contexts, various fuzzy FCA
methods have been proposed [1,2,3,4,6], in which a formal context is extended to
a fuzzy context by replacing the crisp incidence relation with a fuzzy one. The
derivation operators map fuzzy sets on G to fuzzy sets on M and vice versa,
forming a Galois connection. Consequently, fuzzy concepts are pairs of fuzzy
sets mutually determined by these operators. Typically, membership functions
of these fuzzy sets measure the degree to which an attribute (object) connects
with all objects (attributes). However, unlike the existing approaches, we further
define the derivation operators using (strict) c-cuts for c ∈[0, 1] on these fuzzy
sets, mapping a set of objects to the attributes whose degree of connecting with
all these objects meets or exceeds c, and vice versa.
As logics for fuzzy formal contexts are under-explored yet, there exist only a
few works on this topic. Among them, two approaches to connecting logic with
fuzzy formal contexts have been proposed in [7]. One is to follow an idea in [8] by
interpreting fuzzy logic formulas as formal concepts, and the other is to consider
semantic evaluations and formulas of a fuzzy logic as objects and attributes of a
fuzzy context, respectively, and use the fuzzy relation between them to denote
the satisfaction degree of a formula in an evaluation.
In this paper, we present yet another approach based on the weighted exten-
sion of the above-mentioned two-sorted Boolean modal logic. By considering the
fuzzy incidence relation as a class of possibility distributions, we use weighted

Preliminaries
In this section, we introduce some preliminary notions about FCA and possibility

2.1
Formal concept analysis
Let us recall the definition of a context (G, M, I) introduced in the previous
section. A given context induces two derivation operators + : (P(G), ⊆) →
(P(M), ⊇) and −: (P(M), ⊇) →(P(G), ⊆), where for all A ∈P(G) and B ∈
P(M):
A+ = {m ∈M | ∀g(g ∈A ⇒gIm)},
B−= {g ∈G | ∀m(m ∈B ⇒gIm)}.
It can be shown that the pair of maps forms a Galois connection. More-
over, every Galois connection is associated with some formal context, and this
correspondence is bijective. For details, we refer the reader to [16].
A (formal) concept is a pair of sets (A, B) such that A+ = B and A = B−,
where A and B are called its extent and intent, respectively. We denote the set
of all concepts by B(K). It forms a complete lattice and is denoted by B(K).
Düntsch and Gediga [14] defined sufficiency, dual sufficiency, possibility and
necessity operators based on a context. In particular, for a context K := (G, M, I),
A ⊆G, and B ⊆M, the pairs of possibility and necessity operators are defined
as follows:
A3 := {m ∈M | ∃g(gIm ∧g ∈A)},
A2 := {m ∈M | ∀g(gIm ⇒g ∈A)}.
B3 := {g ∈G | ∃m(gIm∧m ∈B)},
B2 := {g ∈G | ∀m(gIm ⇒m ∈B)}.
These operators correspond to approximation operators in rough set theory
(RST) [30]. Based on them, Düntsch and Gediga [14] and Yao [34] introduced
property oriented concepts and object oriented concepts, respectively. A pair
(A, B) is a property oriented concept of K iff A3 = B and B2 = A, and it is

In addition to formal concept lattices mentioned above, the set O(K) of all

property oriented concept lattices, respectively. The relationship among the three
kinds of concept lattices are investigated in [33].
Regarding the motivation of the paper, we use the following example to show
how a formal context can represent a dataset in practical applications.
Example 1 Let us consider a formal context (G, M, I), where G is the set of
readers (users) of an on-line document repository M containing books, articles,
and so on. In such a scenario, for g ∈G and m ∈M, gIm means that the reader
g has downloaded the document m. Assume that A is a particular group of users
and B is a special class of documents. Then, the above-mentioned operators of
FCA may contain the following information:
– g ∈B2: the reader g only downloaded documents in B
– m ∈A2: only readers in A downloaded the document m
– g ∈B−: the reader g downloaded all documents in B
– m ∈A+: all readers in A downloaded the document m
2.2
Fuzzy set, possibility theory, and possibilistic reasoning
Fuzzy set theory is invented by Zadeh [35] to model vagueness in set membership.
A fuzzy set is a set without a precise boundary between its elements and non-
elements. Given a universe W, a fuzzy set ˜A over W is defined as a membership
function ˜A : W →[0, 1]. We denote the class of all fuzzy subsets of a universe W
by ˜P(W). A fuzzy binary relation between two domains U and V is simply a fuzzy
subset of its Cartesian product U ×V . Let ˜A be a fuzzy set over W and c ∈[0, 1].
Then, the c-cut and strict c-cut of ˜A are defined as ˜Ac = {w ∈W | ˜A(w) ≥c}
and ˜A>c = {w ∈W | ˜A(w) > c}, respectively.
When the universe W is a set of possible worlds (or states), we can interpret a
fuzzy set as a possibility distribution . This leads to the development of possibility
theory [36].
Definition 1. [13] Let W be a set. Then, we define
– a possibility distribution over W as a function π : W →[0, 1],
– the possibility measure Π : P(W) →[0, 1] by Π(X) := supx∈X π(x),
– the necessity measure N : P(W) →[0, 1] by N(X) := infx̸∈X(1 −π(x)),
– the guaranteed possibility measure △: P(W) →[0, 1] by △(X) := infx∈X π(x),
– the potential certainty measure ▽: P(W) →[0, 1] by ▽(X) := supx̸∈X(1 −
π(x)).
By mathematical convention, sup ∅= 0 and inf ∅= 1. Hence, by the definition,
we have Π(∅) = ▽(W) = 0 and N(W) =△(∅) = 1.

Fuzzy Formal Context and Cut-based Concepts
Let us recall the scenario in Example 1. Sometimes, the data may contain
vague or more fine-grained information. For example, it may be known that
how strongly a reader is interested in a document. To model such situations,
we introduce the fuzzy formal context, which is a special instance of formal
L-contexts defined in [2], where L is any residuated lattice.
Definition 2. A fuzzy formal context is defined as (G, M, ˜I), where G and M
are the same as above, but ˜I : G × M →[0, 1] is now a fuzzy binary relation.
Intuitively, ˜I(g, m) specifies the strength or intensity of the connection between
g and m.
Given a fuzzy formal context (G, M, ˜I), we can define possibility distributions
πg : M →[0, 1] for each g ∈G and πm : G →[0, 1] for each m ∈M in the
following way:
πg(m) = ˜I(g, m), m ∈M
πm(g) = ˜I(g, m), g ∈G.
The definitions of (·)+, (·)−, and approximation operators in fuzzy formal context
are similar to those for formal context except that the incidence relation I is
replaced by ˜I and logical connectives and quantifiers are replaced by those for
fuzzy logic. Formally, we can use the residuum of Łukasiewicz t-norm [18] to
define these operators. As the Łukasiewicz t-norm ∗: [0, 1]2 →[0, 1] is simply

a ⇒b = 1 −a + b if a > b. Then, we can define corresponding operators in fuzzy

Two-sorted Weighted Modal Logic
To represent and reason with information about fuzzy formal context, we present
the two-sorted weighted modal logic 2WML. The signature of 2WML consists
of two sorts {s1, s2} and two sets of modalities Σs1s2 and Σs2s1 with arities
s1 →s2 and s2 →s1, respectively:
Σs1s2 = {[c]o, [c+]o, [[c]]o, [[c+]]o | c ∈[0, 1]},
Σs2s1 = {[c]p, [c+]p, [[c]]p, [[c+]]p | c ∈[0, 1]}.

Necessity and Sufficiency Fragments of 2WML
While we have established the soundness of 2WML with respect to fuzzy context
models, it remains an open question whether the logic is also complete. However,
we can show that the necessity and sufficiency fragments of 2WML are both
sound and complete with respect to fuzzy context models. The language of the
necessity fragment includes all propositional connectives along with the modal
operators [c] and [c+], whereas the language of the sufficiency fragment is formed
using the modalities [[c]] and [[c+]].
5.1
The system 2WKB
In this section, we discuss the necessity fragment of 2WML. The logic is called
2WKB. Its signature is derived from that of 2WML omitting sufficiency modal-
ities [[c]] and [[c+]]. Syntax and semantics, particularly the definition of formulas
and their satisfaction, are inherited from 2WML without modification. We use

rules (a) and (b) of 2WML. Also, we still use |= and ⊢to denote the seman-

2WKB is a weighted extension of the two-sorted modal logic KB introduced
in [22,23] and a two-sorted variant of the quantitative modal logic (QML) in-
troduced in [26]. Hence, we can prove the completeness of 2WKB employing a
synergy of the canonical model construction techniques used in KB and QML.
As in QML, 2WKB lacks the compactness theorem in its semantics. That
is, a finitely satisfiable set of formulas is not necessarily satisfiable in 2WKB.
However, since the derivation in an axiomatic system is always finitary, we can
not have the completeness without any restriction. Hence, we restrict our con-

formulas. For this, we define the set of degrees occurring (explicitly or implicitly)

We can now state the main theorem of this section.
Theorem 3. 2WKB is sound and complete with respect to the class of all
fuzzy context models. That is for any s ∈{s1, s2}, Γ ⊢s ϕ iff Γ |=s ϕ where

Proof. The soundness follows from that of 2WML. Here, we simply sketch the
proof of the completeness and its details are included in Appendix B. Suppose
that Γ ̸⊢s ϕ. Hence, Γ ∪{¬ϕ} is consistent. By the standard Lindenbaum lemma,
Γ ∪{¬ϕ} can be extended to a maximally consistent subset Φs of s-sorted for-

domains are the sets of all maximally consistent subsets. The model satisfies the
truth lemma, that is, a formula is true in an element of the domains iff it belongs
to that element. Thus, we have a model and its domain element Φs in which all
formulas in Γ is true but ϕ is false, i.e. Γ ̸|=s ϕ.⊣
To construct the canonical model, let us consider the set of two-sorted formu-

Extension to Multi-Relational Fuzzy Contexts
In this section, we demonstrate the possibility of extending the logic to reasoning
with Boolean combinations of relations in multi-relational fuzzy contexts.
In classical modal logic, when both necessity and sufficiency modalities are
available in a logical language, it can be extended to express all Boolean com-
binations of multiple modalities. This leads to Boolean modal logic in
[17].
Semantically, each primitive modality in Boolean modal logic is interpreted with
respect to a binary accessibility relation in Kripke models and Boolean combi-
nations of modalities correspond to the set-theoretic Boolean algebra of multiple
relations. In particular, sufficiency modalities correspond to complements of bi-
nary relations.
Analogously, we can also extend 2WML to a kind of two-sorted weighted
Boolean modal logic (2WBML). Let Π0 be a set of primitive modality indices
and let cl(Π0) be its inductive closure over union, intersection, and complement.
Formally, an index in cl(Π0) is defined by
i ::= 0 | a | i ∩i | i ∪i | i,
where a ∈Π0. Then, we modify the signature of 2WML by attaching to each
modality an index from cl(Π0). Thus, for example, we have a 2WBML for-
mula of the form [c]i∩j
o φ. Semantically, these formulas are interpreted in multi-
relational fuzzy context models M = (G, M, (˜Ii)i∈cl(Π0), v), where G, M, and v
are defined as in fuzzy context models, and each ˜Ii : G × M →[0, 1] is a fuzzy
binary relation such that for any g ∈G and m ∈M
1. ˜I0(g, m) = 0
2. ˜Ii∩j(g, m) = min(˜Ii(g, m), ˜Ij(g, m))
3. ˜Ii∪j(g, m) = max(˜Ii(g, m), ˜Ij(g, m))
4. ˜Ii(g, m) = 1 −˜Ii(g, m).
To obtain axiomatization of 2WBML, we simply replace modalities in the
2WML system with indexed ones and add the extra axioms and rule as fol-
lows:

7
Concluding Remarks
We have introduced a 2-sorted weighted modal logic for reasoning with fuzzy
formal contexts. Our illustrative examples demonstrate its potential applicabil-
ity to the representation of knowledge discovered from fuzzy databases. As the
logic is at the early stage of development yet, there are a lot of open issues to be
addressed. First, the most pressing issue is the completeness of the axiomatiza-
tion for 2WML and 2WBML. Second, while 2WML is still a two-valued logic,
more generally, we may also study genuine many-valued modal logic following
the mathematical fuzzy logic paradigm initiated by Hájek [18]. In addition, con-
sidering the frequency of connections between objects and attributes, we can
also study probabilistic reasoning [29] about formal contexts. Last, to apply
the formalism to practical data mining problem, we will need to implement its
automated reasoning system and test it on some real datasets.
