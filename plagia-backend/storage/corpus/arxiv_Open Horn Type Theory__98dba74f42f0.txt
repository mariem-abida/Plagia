CHAPTER 1
The Calculus
Gap is not absence. Gap is witness.
— First principle
We begin with a claim: the fundamental asymmetry in logic—that
truth is constructed while falsity is merely the absence of construc-
tion—is not necessary. It is a choice, inherited from the intuitionistic
tradition, that forecloses an entire dimension of meaning.
In Martin-Löf Type Theory and its homotopical descendant, a
type is inhabited or not. A path exists or does not. The machinery
has no way to say: I have witnessed that this path does not exist. It can
only say: I have not (yet) found this path, or, in the classical shadow,
assuming this path exists leads to absurdity.
Open Horn Type Theory makes a different choice. Coherence
and gap are both primitive. Both can be witnessed. Both carry
proof-relevant structure. Neither is derived from the other.
What follows is the calculus in its minimal form: the judgments,
the single law, the derived notion of horn, and the relationship to
dependent type theory. Everything else that follows builds on this
foundation.
1.1
Judgments with Polarity
Let J range over judgments. In a type-theoretic setting, these include
formation judgments (A type), typing judgments (t : A), and equality
judgments (s ≡t : A). But the calculus is not restricted to type theory;
J may be any judgment form in any formal system.
Let Γ be a context—a finite, consistent collection of assumptions
and prior witnesses.
3

CHAPTER 1. THE CALCULUS
Definition 1.1 (Witnessed Judgments). The Open Horn Type Theory
has two primitive judgment forms:
Γ ⊢+ J
Γ ⊢−J
The first is read: “J is witnessed coherent in context Γ.” The second:
“J is witnessed gapped in context Γ.”
Both forms are proof-relevant. If γ : Γ ⊢+ J and γ′ : Γ ⊢+ J, we do
not assume γ = γ′. Different witnesses to the same coherence may
carry different structure. The same holds for gap witnesses: ω and
ω′ witnessing the same gap may differ.
The terminology is deliberate. We say coherent rather than “true”
or “derivable” because the judgment is not merely valid—it is wit-
nessed as holding together. We say gapped rather than “false” or “re-
futed” because the judgment is not negated—it is witnessed as failing
to cohere. This is not a distinction without difference.
Definition 1.2 (The Open). A judgment J is open in context Γ if Γ
contains neither a coherence witness for J nor a gap witness for J.
The open is not a third truth value. It is not “unknown” in any
epistemic sense. It is the space of what has not yet been witnessed—the
locus of potential judgment, where coherence or gap may eventually
be established, but where neither currently stands.
APPLICATION.
When we read a text to understand its sense,
open judgments are the places where attention has not yet arrived.
The text presents itself through acts of witnessing—coherence here,
gap there. What remains unwitnessed is not nothing; it is the
not-yet-known, the space where meaning may still be determined.
1.1.1
The Exclusion Law
The only axiom of the Open Horn Type Theory governs the relation-
ship between coherence and gap:
Definition 1.3 (Exclusion).
Γ ⊢+ J
Γ ⊢−J
⊥
Coherence and gap, for the same judgment in the same context, are
mutually exclusive.
4

1.1. JUDGMENTS WITH POLARITY
This is the entire axiomatic content of the calculus. No other
structural rules are imposed. In particular:
• We do not assume that every judgment is either coherent or
gapped (no excluded middle).
• We do not assume that coherence for J implies gap for ¬J (no
duality).
• We do not assume that gap is “negation” in any classical or
intuitionistic sense.
Gap is positive structure. A gap witness ω : Γ ⊢−J is evidence for
the non-coherence of J, not evidence against it. The distinction mat-
ters: classical negation is the claim that coherence leads to absurdity;
intuitionistic negation is a function from coherence proofs to ⊥; gap
is neither. Gap is: I have witnessed that this does not cohere.
1.1.2
Contrast with Negation
Let us be precise about what gap is not.
Classical negation.
In classical logic, ¬P is defined such that P ∨
¬P holds universally. Negation is the complement of truth. But in
OHTT, there is no assumption that J is either coherent or gapped.
The open is a genuine third state—not a truth value, but a witnessing
state.
Intuitionistic negation.
In intuitionistic logic, ¬P is defined as
P →⊥. A proof of ¬P is a function that transforms any proof of P
into a proof of absurdity. This is constructive, but it is still derivative:
negation is defined in terms of implication and absurdity, not given
primitively. In OHTT, gap is primitive. We do not define Γ ⊢−J as
“Γ ⊢+ J implies ⊥.” We assert it directly.
Paraconsistent approaches.
Some logics allow both P and ¬P to
hold without explosion. OHTT is not paraconsistent. The Exclu-
sion Law explicitly forbids simultaneous coherence and gap for
the same judgment. What OHTT permits is that neither holds—the
open—which paraconsistent logics do not typically thematize.
5

The closest relatives are perhaps bilattice logics (Belnap, Ginsberg)
which track both truth and information, or signed logics with explicit
polarity. But OHTT differs in its proof-relevance: we track not just
that a judgment is coherent or gapped, but how—the witness carries
structure.
1.2
Horns as Compositional Tension
The judgment forms and the Exclusion Law constitute the entire
axiomatic structure of OHTT. From here, we derive the characteristic
construction: the horn.
Let ⇒denote a compositional relation between judgments. In
different instantiations of OHTT, this may be:
• Derivability (in proof theory): J ⇒K if K is derivable from J
• Substitution (in type theory): J ⇒K if K results from substituting
into J
• Transport (in HoTT): J ⇒K if K is the transported judgment
along a path
• Semantic entailment: J ⇒K if the meaning of J implies the
meaning of K
The specific choice of ⇒depends on the application domain.
What matters is that ⇒has a compositional character: if J ⇒K and
K ⇒L, then we may ask whether J ⇒L.
Definition 1.4 (The Horn). For judgments J, K, L and compositional
relation ⇒, the open horn type is:
Λ(J, K, L) := (Γ ⊢+ J ⇒K) × (Γ ⊢+ K ⇒L) × (Γ ⊢−J ⇒L)
An inhabitant of Λ(J, K, L) is a triple (γ1, γ2, ω) where γ1 witnesses
that J composes to K, γ2 witnesses that K composes to L, and ω
witnesses that the direct composition from J to L is gapped.
The horn is data, not failure. In ordinary category theory or
type theory, if we have f : A →B and g : B →C, the composite
g ◦f : A →C is assumed to exist. Composition is total. The horn
records the situation where composition is witnessed blocked: we have
the two steps, but the closure is gapped.
6

1.2. HORNS AS COMPOSITIONAL TENSION
1.2.1
Geometric Intuition
The terminology “horn” comes from simplicial homotopy theory. A
2-simplex (triangle) has three vertices and three edges:
K
J
L
γ2
γ1
ω
A horn Λ2
1 consists of two edges of a potential triangle—here,
γ1 : J →K and γ2 : K →L—without the third edge. In a Kan complex
(the standard simplicial model for spaces), every horn can be filled:
the missing edge can always be supplied. This is the Kan condition.
In OHTT, we drop this assumption. Instead, the third edge may
be:
• Coherently filled: there exists γ : Γ ⊢+ J ⇒L completing the
triangle
• Gap-witnessed: there exists ω : Γ ⊢−J ⇒L, and we have a
horn Λ(J, K, L)
• Open: neither coherence nor gap is witnessed for J ⇒L
The horn Λ(J, K, L) is inhabited precisely in the second case: two
coherent steps, one gapped closure. This is structural tension made
formal.
APPLICATION.
A horn in a text is the record of local coherence
with global rupture. Two sentences may each cohere with a third,
while the direct path between them is witnessed as gapped. Three
theories may be pairwise consistent but globally inconsistent. The
horn does not merely note that something is missing; it records the
specific structure of what coheres and what does not.
1.2.2
Higher Horns
The construction iterates. Given coherence witnesses γ, γ′ : Γ ⊢+ J,
we may ask whether γ and γ′ themselves cohere—whether there is a
“path between paths.”
7

Define the level-1 judgment type as coherence witnesses them-
selves:
J1 := Σ(J : Judgment). (Γ ⊢+ J)
Then we instantiate OHTT again at level 1, with J1 as the judg-
ment type. This gives:
• Coherence between coherences: η : Γ ⊢+ (γ ⇒γ′)
• Gap between coherences: ν : Γ ⊢−(γ ⇒γ′)
• Level-1 horns: Λ1(γ, γ′, γ′′)
This is exactly the structure of homotopy type theory’s higher
identity types—but with gap at every level. In HoTT, paths be-
tween paths are homotopies; homotopies between homotopies are
2-homotopies; and so on. In OHTT, at each level we have coherence,
gap, and open.
The construction is fractal: the same calculus, applied recursively,
generates the full tower of higher structure. This is the sense in
which OHTT is a metalogic: it provides the scaffolding within which
ordinary type theories live as fragments.
1.3
The Coherent Fragment
We now make precise the relationship between OHTT and dependent
type theory.
Definition 1.5 (The Coherent Fragment). The coherent fragment of
OHTT is the subcalculus that uses only the judgment form Γ ⊢+ J,
never Γ ⊢−J.
In the coherent fragment:
• The Exclusion Law is trivially satisfied (no ⊢−judgments to
conflict)
• Horns cannot be inhabited (they require a ⊢−component)
• All unwitnessed judgments are open (gap is unavailable)
8

1.3. THE COHERENT FRAGMENT
Definition 1.6 (MLTT as Coherent Fragment). Let the judgment
forms of Martin-Löf Type Theory be:
Γ ctx
(Γ is a well-formed context)
Γ ⊢A type
(A is a type in context Γ)
Γ ⊢t : A
(t has type A in context Γ)
Γ ⊢s ≡t : A
(s and t are definitionally equal)
The rules of MLTT (Π-formation, Π-introduction, Π-elimination, Σ-
types, identity types, universes, etc.) are coherence constructors: rules
that generate ⊢+ judgments from ⊢+ premises.
This is not an encoding of MLTT into OHTT. It is not a translation
or model. It is the observation that MLTT is the coherent fragment of
OHTT—the part that uses only the right hand.
Working entirely within the coherent fragment, a mathematician
derives judgments using the standard rules. They may attempt
to inhabit a type and fail, but they cannot witness the failure—the
failure is absence, not structure. OHTT extends this by making failure
structurable.
1.3.1
HoTT as Coherent Fragment with Higher Structure
Homotopy Type Theory extends MLTT with:
• Univalence: type equivalence induces identity, (A ≃B) ≃
(A =U B)
• Higher inductive types: types specified with path constructors,
not just point constructors
• The homotopy interpretation: types are spaces, identity types
are path spaces
In the simplicial semantics of HoTT, types are interpreted as
Kan complexes—simplicial sets where every horn fills. This is the
Kan condition. Paths (inhabitants of identity types) are 1-simplices;
homotopies are 2-simplices; and so on.
OHTT generalizes this. The coherent fragment with higher struc-
ture corresponds to HoTT: paths are level-0 coherence witnesses,
homotopies are level-1 coherence witnesses. But OHTT allows gap
witnesses at each level, breaking the Kan condition.
9

HoTT = Coherent Fragment of OHTT + Higher Structure + Kan Filling
OHTT = HoTT −Kan Filling + Gap Witnesses
1.3.2
What OHTT Adds
OHTT extends the coherent fragment by introducing:
1. Witnessed typing failures.
In MLTT, if we cannot derive t : A,
we simply lack a derivation. In OHTT, we can witness Γ ⊢−(t :
A)—the judgment that t does not have type A. This is not “t has
some other type” or “the derivation fails.” It is positive evidence of
non-typing.
2. Witnessed non-identity.
In HoTT, ¬(x =A y) is defined as
(x =A y) →⊥. This says: any path from x to y leads to absurdity.
In OHTT, we can witness Γ ⊢−(p : x =A y) directly—the judgment
that x and y are gapped. This is stronger: not “a path would be
catastrophic” but “we have witnessed non-coherence.”
3. Transport horns.
Given t : B(x) and p : x =A y, HoTT guar-
antees transport(p, t) : B(y). In OHTT, transport may be gapped:
Γ ⊢−(transport(p, t) : B(y)). This is the transport horn—the central
example of Chapter II—where local coherence (the term exists, the
path exists) meets global rupture (the transported term does not
cohere).
4. Horn structure at all levels.
Whenever two coherence witnesses
compose but their composition is gapped, we have a horn. This struc-
ture exists at every level of the type-theoretic hierarchy, recording
where local derivability fails to compose globally.
1.4
Summary
The Open Horn Type Theory consists of:
• Two judgment forms: Γ ⊢+ J (coherence witness) and Γ ⊢−J
(gap witness)
10

1.4. SUMMARY
• One axiom: Exclusion (coherence and gap for the same judg-
ment yield ⊥)
• One derived structure: The horn Λ(J, K, L) recording two co-
herent steps with a gapped closure
• One relationship: Dependent type theory is the coherent (⊢+-
only) fragment
Everything else—the simplicial semantics of Part II, the obstruc-
tion examples of Part III, the dynamics and persistence of later chap-
ters—builds on this foundation without extending it. The calculus is
complete.
What makes this a new logic is not complexity but reorientation.
By taking gap as primitive, we gain the ability to structure what does
not cohere—to witness rupture, not merely fail to witness coherence.
This is a small change with large consequences.
APPLICATION.
What this lets us say that HoTT cannot: “I
have witnessed that these two paths do not cohere—not that their
coherence would be catastrophic, but that the space between them is
structured absence.” The open horn is not failure. It is the shape of
what does not close.

Horn
The path exists. The term exists.
What does not exist is their meeting.
— The transport horn
In Chapter I we gave the abstract calculus: two judgment forms,
one exclusion law, and the derived notion of horn. But a calculus
without examples is a skeleton without flesh. We now show that the
transport horn—the situation where a term and a path both exist,
but the transported term does not cohere—is the central construction
that distinguishes OHTT from HoTT.
This chapter has three movements. First, we recall transport in
ordinary type theory and its simplicial semantics. Second, we define
the transport horn and show that it cannot be expressed in HoTT.
Third, we give three classes of examples—topological, semantic,
and logical—that demonstrate the phenomenon is not artificial but
ubiquitous.
2.1
Transport in Homotopy Type Theory
We begin with the standard picture.
Definition 2.1 (Type Family). A type family over A is a function
B : A →U assigning to each x : A a type B(x).
In the homotopy interpretation, a type family is a fibration: B
assigns to each point of the base space A a fiber, and the fibers vary
continuously (in an appropriate sense) as we move through A.
13

Definition 2.2 (Transport). Given a type family B : A →U, a term
t : B(x), and a path p : x =A y, the transport of t along p is a term:
transport(p, t) : B(y)
In HoTT, transport is not merely definable—it is derivable from
the induction principle for identity types. The J-rule guarantees: for any
type family B over A, any x : A, and any t : B(x), if we have a path
p : x =A y, we can construct a term of type B(y).
This is the fundamental operation that makes dependent types
“work” in the homotopical setting. Without transport, a term in B(x)
would be trapped at x, unable to move along paths. With transport,
terms flow through the type family as we traverse the base.
APPLICATION.
The power of transport is also its danger. In
HoTT, transport always succeeds. Given any path, any term moves.
But this universality obscures situations where transport should
fail—where the path exists, the term exists, but their combination
does not cohere. OHTT makes such failures visible.

2.2
The Transport Horn
We now define the central construction of OHTT.
Definition 2.5 (Transport Horn). Given a type family B : A →U, a
term t : B(x), a path p : x =A y, and a context Γ, the transport horn
is an inhabitant of the type:
Λtr(B, t, p) := Γ ⊢+ (t : B(x))
× Γ ⊢+ (p : x =A y)
× Γ ⊢−(transport(p, t) : B(y))
An inhabitant (γt, γp, ω) of the transport horn witnesses:
• γt: the term t coherently inhabits B(x)
• γp: the path p coherently connects x to y
• ω: the transported term transport(p, t) is gapped at B(y)

This is precisely the situation that HoTT cannot express. In HoTT,
given γt and γp, the term transport(p, t) : B(y) is derivable. There is
no way to assert that transport fails; the rules guarantee it succeeds.
In OHTT, we can have coherent premises and a gapped conclu-
sion. The horn records the structural tension: local coherence (the
term is well-typed, the path is well-formed) with global rupture
(their combination does not cohere).
2.2.1

What does a transport horn look like simplicially?

2.2.2
Relation to the General Horn
The transport horn is an instance of the general horn schema from
Chapter I. Recall:
Λ(J, K, L) := (Γ ⊢+ J ⇒K) × (Γ ⊢+ K ⇒L) × (Γ ⊢−J ⇒L)
For transport, instantiate:
J := (t : B(x))
K := (p : x =A y)
L := (transport(p, t) : B(y))
J ⇒K := “t and p are composable data for transport”
K ⇒L := “p determines the target type B(y) for transport”
J ⇒L := “t transports along p to inhabit B(y)”
The transport horn asserts: the first two compositions cohere (we
have the data), but the third is gapped (the transport itself fails).
2.3
The Ruptured Simplicial Model
To give semantics to OHTT, we generalize the simplicial model of
HoTT.
Definition 2.6 (Ruptured Simplicial Set). A ruptured simplicial set
is a tuple (X, Coh, Gap, Excl) where:
• X is a graded set X = (X0, X1, X2, . . .) with face and degeneracy
maps
• Cohn ⊆Xn is the set of coherently witnessed n-simplices
• Gapn ⊆{horns in X} is the set of gap-witnessed horns
• Excl: if h ∈Gapn, then no σ ∈Cohn fills h
The key difference from ordinary simplicial sets: we track not
just which simplices exist, but which are witnessed as coherent, and
which horns are witnessed as unfillable.
Definition 2.7 (Ruptured Kan Complex). A ruptured simplicial set
(X, Coh, Gap, Excl) is a ruptured Kan complex if for every horn h in
X, exactly one of the following holds:

1. There exists σ ∈Coh filling h (the horn is coherently filled)
2. h ∈Gap (the horn is gap-witnessed)
3. Neither (the horn is open)
An ordinary Kan complex is a ruptured Kan complex where
Gap = ∅and every horn has a coherent filler. HoTT lives in this
special case. OHTT lives in the general case.
Definition 2.8 (Ruptured Kan Fibration). A map p : E →B of
ruptured simplicial sets is a ruptured Kan fibration if the lifting
property holds up to the gap structure: for every lifting problem, either
a coherent lift exists, or the lifting horn is gap-witnessed, or the
problem is open.
Proposition 2.9 (Transport in Ruptured Fibrations). In a ruptured Kan
fibration p : E →B, given t ∈Ex and γ : x →y in B, the transport of t
along γ is:
1. Coherent if the lifting horn has a coherent filler
2. Gapped if the lifting horn is gap-witnessed
3. Open if the lifting horn is neither
This proposition is the semantic content of the transport horn.
OHTT allows case (2); HoTT forbids it.
2.4
Three Classes of Examples
We now give concrete examples of transport horns in three domains:
topology, semantics, and logic. These are previews of the detailed
case studies in Part III.
2.4.1
Topological Obstruction: Monodromy
Let p : E →B be a covering space (a particularly nice kind of
fibration). Choose a base point x ∈B and consider a loop γ : x →x
in B.
If t ∈Ex is a point in the fiber, we can try to lift γ to a path in E
starting at t. For covering spaces, the lift exists and is unique. But
18

2.4. THREE CLASSES OF EXAMPLES
the lifted path may not return to t—it may end at a different point
t′ ∈Ex.
The monodromy of the covering is the permutation of the fiber
induced by lifting loops.
Example 2.10 (Möbius Strip). Let B = S1 (the circle) and let E be the
boundary of a Möbius strip—a double cover of S1. The fiber over
any point has two elements: call them + and −.
The intuition is immediate: walk once around the base circle,
starting at a point b. Downstairs, you return to b—the loop closes.
But upstairs, if you start at + and walk, you arrive at −. You come
back changed.
Mathematically: the lift of the loop exists and is unique (it’s the
path starting at +). But it is not a loop based at +—it ends at −. The
question “does this loop lift to a loop at my starting point?” has a
definite negative answer, and the monodromy (the sheet-swap) is
the certificate.
This is a transport horn:
• The starting point + ∈Eb is coherent (it exists)
• The loop γ : b →b is coherent (it exists)
• The based lift is gapped (it does not exist as a loop at +)
The gap witness is the monodromy: not silence about whether a
lift exists, but positive data recording how the lift fails to close. The
deck transformation that swaps + and −is the obstruction made
structural.
APPLICATION.
The Möbius strip is the simplest non-trivial exam-
ple of this phenomenon. The twist is not just a curiosity—it is a gap
witness. OHTT lets us say: walking around the base is coherent,
your starting point is coherent, but returning to where you started
is gapped. The formalism holds what the image shows.
2.4.2
Semantic Obstruction: Polysemy
Consider natural language as a type-theoretic structure. Let:
• A = the type of word tokens (occurrences of words in context)

• B(w) = the type of meanings (senses) of word w
Two tokens of the “same word” are related by a path in A. The
word “bank” in a financial context and “bank” in a river context are
connected by lexical identity—they are the same lexeme.
Let x = “bank” (financial) and y = “bank” (river). Let p : x =A y
witness their lexical identity.
Let t : B(x) be the meaning “financial institution.”
What is transport(p, t)? It would be: the meaning of “bank” (river)
obtained by transporting the financial meaning along the lexical
identity path.
But this doesn’t cohere. The river sense of “bank” is not “financial
institution.” The lexical identity exists, but it does not transport
meanings.
Example 2.11 (Polysemy Horn).
Λtr(B, “financial institution”, plexical)
is inhabited by:
• γt: witnessing that “financial institution” is a coherent meaning
of “bank” (financial)
• γp: witnessing that “bank” (financial) and “bank” (river) are
lexically identical
• ω: witnessing that “financial institution” is not a coherent mean-
ing of “bank” (river)
In HoTT, if we had a path p : x = y, transport would force
meanings to transfer. We would be unable to model polysemy—the
fact that the same word has genuinely different meanings that do
not reduce to each other.
In OHTT, the transport horn records exactly this: lexical identity
without semantic identity. The path exists. The meaning exists. But
the meaning does not travel along the path.
APPLICATION.
A natural question: how would one actually mea-
sure whether meanings cohere? Distributional semantics provides
an answer. In word embedding models—and in the attention mech-
anisms of large language models—words live in high-dimensional

vector spaces where semantic similarity becomes geometric proxim-

but their contextual embeddings land in distant regions of meaning-

The transport horn formalizes this divergence: lexical proximity

2.4.3
Logical Obstruction: Underivability
Consider a dependent type theory as a category with families. Let:
• A = the type of contexts
• B(Γ) = the type of judgments derivable in context Γ
A context morphism σ : ∆→Γ (a substitution) induces a path
pσ : Γ =A ∆in an appropriate sense.
Given a judgment J derivable in Γ, we can ask: is J[σ] (the substi-
tuted judgment) derivable in ∆?
In standard type theory, substitution preserves derivability. If
Γ ⊢t : T, then ∆⊢t[σ] : T[σ]. This is the substitution lemma.
But consider a richer setting where derivability is resource-sensitive,
or where contexts carry additional structure. Then substitution may
fail to preserve derivability.
Example 2.12 (Resource Sensitivity). Let Γ = x : File, y : ReadPerm(x)
and let J = read(x) : Data.
Let σ : ∆→Γ be a substitution where ∆provides the file but not
the read permission.
Then:
• ⊢+ Γ ⊢J: the read is derivable with permission
• ⊢+ (σ : ∆→Γ): the substitution is well-formed
• ⊢−∆⊢J[σ]: the read is not derivable without permission

This is a transport horn: the judgment is derivable, the substitu-
tion exists, but the substituted judgment is gapped.
The gap witness here is not merely “no derivation found” but a

APPLICATION.
This connects OHTT to certified compilation,
proof-carrying code, and information flow analysis. Systems that
track permissions, capabilities, or security levels routinely encounter
situations where operations are well-typed in one context but blocked
in another. The transport horn is the natural formalization: substi-
tution exists, but derivability does not transport.
2.5
The Functoriality Question
In HoTT, transport is functorial. Given paths p : x =A y and q : y =A
z, we have:
transport(q · p, t) = transport(q, transport(p, t))
Transport along a composite path equals iterated transport.
In OHTT, this equation becomes a question. We may have:
• transport(p, t) coherent
• transport(q, transport(p, t)) coherent
• But transport(q · p, t) gapped—or open
The composite path may fail to transport even when the individ-
ual segments succeed.
Example 2.13 (Cumulative Obstruction). In the polysemy example,
consider three senses of “crane”: the bird, the machine, and the
poetic verb (to crane one’s neck).
• Path p: bird →machine (both are tall, thin, with a hook-like
part)
• Path q: machine →verb (the machine’s motion resembles neck-
ing craning)

2.6. WHAT THIS LETS US SAY
Some semantic features transport along p (tallness, thinness).
Some transport along q (extension, reaching). But transporting di-
rectly from bird to verb via q · p may lose all coherent features—the
composite obstruction is worse than the sum of parts.
This failure of functoriality is not a bug but a feature. It captures
the phenomenon of semantic drift: meanings can travel stepwise
through intermediate senses but lose coherence over longer paths.
Definition 2.14 (Functoriality Horn). Given paths p : x =A y and
q : y =A z, and a term t : B(x), the functoriality horn is:
Λfunc(t, p, q) := Γ ⊢+ (transport(p, t) : B(y))
× Γ ⊢+ (transport(q, (transport(p, t))) : B(z))
× Γ ⊢−(transport(q · p, t) : B(z))
The functoriality horn witnesses: stepwise transport succeeds,
but direct transport along the composite fails. This is a higher-order
obstruction invisible to HoTT.
2.6
What This Lets Us Say
Let us be explicit about the expressive gain.
In HoTT, we can say:
• “t has type B(x)”
• “p is a path from x to y”
• “transport(p, t) has type B(y)” (automatically, by the rules)
• “transport(p, t) = s” for some specific s : B(y)
In HoTT, we cannot say:
• “Transport of t along p is blocked”
• “There is an obstruction to transport” (as positive data)
• “The path exists but does not carry this term”

In OHTT, we can additionally say:
• Γ ⊢−(transport(p, t) : B(y)) — “Transport is witnessed as
blocked”
• Λtr(B, t, p) inhabited — “We have a transport horn”
• The obstruction ω carries structure—why transport fails
This is the fundamental extension. HoTT can express what trans-
ports; OHTT can also express what does not—and why.
2.7
Summary
The transport horn is the keystone of OHTT:
• Definition: Λtr(B, t, p) =⊢+ (t : B(x))× ⊢+ (p : x = y)× ⊢−
(transport(p, t) : B(y))
• Geometry: Transport is horn-filling. The transport horn is a
horn that is witnessed unfillable.
• Semantics: Ruptured Kan fibrations allow lifting problems to
be coherent, gapped, or open.
• Examples: Monodromy in covering spaces (topological), pol-
ysemy in language (semantic), resource-sensitive derivability
(logical).
• Higher structure: Functoriality may fail; composite paths may
be gapped even when segments transport.
The transport horn is not an exotic edge case. It is the generic
situation whenever paths (identities, equivalences, substitutions)
exist between points whose internal structure does not respect those
paths. HoTT assumes this never happens. OHTT allows it, and gives
it structure.
APPLICATION.
What this lets us say that HoTT cannot: “The
word is the same, but the meaning does not follow. The path is
there, but the term cannot walk it. The identity exists—and yet,
something refuses to be identified.” The transport horn is the formal
shape of that refusal.

The exclusion condition is preserved by this logic.

The lift exists and is unique. But—and this is the key point—the

Going around twice returns to the starting point.

p

The word is the same.

— Polysemy
We now turn to a less traditional domain: the formal semantics
of language. The phenomenon of polysemy—where a single lexical
form carries multiple, non-reducible meanings—is a transport horn.
Lexical identity provides a path; meanings are fibers; the failure of
meaning to transport along lexical identity is witnessed as gap. This
chapter develops the mathematical framework.
6.1
The Semantic Fibration
We model meaning as a fibration over form.
Definition 6.1 (Lexical Space). A lexical space is a simplicial set L
whose:
• 0-simplices are lexical tokens—occurrences of words in context
• 1-simplices are lexical relations—paths connecting tokens
• Higher simplices record coherence of lexical relations
The key lexical relation is identity: two tokens of “the same word.”
We write ℓ∼ℓ′ when ℓand ℓ′ are tokens of the same lexeme.
Definition 6.2 (Meaning Fibration). A meaning fibration is a Kan
fibration p : M →L where:
• M is the meaning space

• For each token ℓ∈L0, the fiber Mℓ= p−1(ℓ) is the space of
meanings (senses) of ℓ
• Paths in M are meaning relations—coherences between senses
In the standard (non-ruptured) setting, the Kan fibration property
guarantees: if γ : ℓ→ℓ′ is a lexical path and m ∈Mℓis a meaning,
then there exists a meaning m′ ∈Mℓ′ connected to m by a path lifting
γ.
This is where polysemy becomes problematic.
6.1.1
The Polysemy Problem
Example 6.3 (Bank). Consider the English word “bank.” Let:
• ℓ1 = token of “bank” in “I deposited money at the bank”
• ℓ2 = token of “bank” in “We picnicked on the river bank”
There is a lexical path γ : ℓ1 →ℓ2 (they are tokens of the same
lexeme).
Let m1 ∈Mℓ1 be the meaning “financial institution.”
What is transport(γ, m1)? The Kan condition would require a
meaning m2 ∈Mℓ2 connected to m1. But “financial institution” is
not a meaning of “bank” in the river context. The meanings are
genuinely disjoint.
The Kan fibration model fails for polysemy. There is no coherent
way to transport “financial institution” along the lexical identity
path.

The Ruptured Meaning Fibration
We now apply OHTT.
Definition 6.4 (Ruptured Meaning Fibration). A ruptured meaning

• L = (L, CohL, GapL) is a ruptured simplicial set of lexical tokens
• M = (M, CohM, GapM) is a ruptured simplicial set of meanings
• Lifting problems (m, γ) may be coherent, gapped, or open

Logical Obstruction
The substitution is valid.

We turn finally to logic itself. The phenomenon of certified under-
ivability—where we can prove that a judgment is not derivable, not
merely that we have failed to derive it—is a transport horn. Substitu-
tions provide paths between contexts; derivable judgments are fibers;
the failure of derivability to transport along substitution is witnessed
as gap. This chapter develops the framework for resource-sensitive
and substructural logics.
7.1
Derivability as Fibration
We begin by modeling the structure of a type theory or logic as a
fibration.
Definition 7.1 (Context Category). Let C be the category of contexts
for a type theory, with:
• Objects: contexts Γ, ∆, Ξ, . . .
• Morphisms: substitutions σ : ∆→Γ (context morphisms)
Substitutions act on judgments: if Γ ⊢J is a judgment in context
Γ and σ : ∆→Γ is a substitution, then J[σ] is the judgment in context
∆obtained by applying σ.
Definition 7.2 (Derivability Fibration). The derivability fibration is
a functor D : Cop →Set (or Kan for the simplicial version) where:
• D(Γ) is the set (or space) of judgments derivable in context Γ

• For σ : ∆→Γ, the map D(σ) : D(Γ) →D(∆) sends J to J[σ]
For a well-behaved type theory (MLTT, HoTT), the derivability
fibration is a “Kan object”: substitution preserves derivability. This
is the content of the substitution lemma.
Lemma 7.3 (Substitution). In MLTT: if Γ ⊢t : A and σ : ∆→Γ is a

The substitution lemma says: derivability transports along substi-

7.2
Resource-Sensitive Failure
The substitution lemma fails in resource-sensitive logics.
Definition 7.4 (Resource-Sensitive Context). A resource-sensitive
context is a context Γ where variables carry resource annotations:
• Linear: must be used exactly once
• Affine: may be used at most once
• Relevant: must be used at least once
• Exponential: may be used arbitrarily (the standard case)
In linear type theory, a substitution σ : ∆→Γ may fail to preserve
derivability if it duplicates or discards linear resources.
Example 7.5 (Linear Failure). Let Γ = x : A (linear) and let J =
(x, x) : A × A.
In ordinary type theory, J is derivable if Γ = x : A. But in linear
type theory, J requires using x twice—violating linearity. So Γ ⊢J is
not derivable.
Now let σ : ∆→Γ where ∆= y : A, z : A and σ = [x 7→y].
Then J[σ] = (y, y) : A × A is still underivable in ∆—we still use
y twice.
But consider σ′ : ∆→Γ with σ′ = [x 7→y] and ∆′ = y : A
(exponential). Now J[σ′] = (y, y) is derivable in ∆′.
The point: derivability depends on resource structure, not just
the term.

When a type theory has decidable type-checking, underivability can

Theorem 7.22 (Decidability Implies Gap Witnesses). Let T be a type

• Substructural restrictions create systematic gaps: failures of

• Modal constraints create transport horns: modality-respecting

• Constraint violations are gap witnesses: substitutions that vio-

• Truncation creates obstructions: escape from propositional trun-

— The relation
We now make precise the relationship between Open Horn Type
Theory and Homotopy Type Theory. The slogan is simple: HoTT is
the coherent fragment of OHTT. But the details illuminate what each
theory can and cannot express.
8.1
The Relationship
OHTT and HoTT share the same raw syntax of dependent type
theory, but differ in what they guarantee:
1. HoTT includes total transport as a theorem: given t : B(x) and
p : x =A y, the term transport(p, t) : B(y) is always derivable.
This corresponds semantically to the Kan filling condition.
2. OHTT does not assume total transport. Transport may suc-
ceed (coherent), fail with witness (gapped), or be undetermined
(open). This corresponds to dropping the Kan condition.
HoTT is thus recovered from OHTT by adjoining a totality principle
ensuring all relevant horns have coherent fillers.
This is the precise sense of the slogan: OHTT = HoTT −Kan
Filling + Gap Witnesses.
8.1.1
The Coherent Fragment
Definition 8.1 (Coherent Fragment). The coherent fragment of OHTT
is the subcategory OHTT+ consisting of:

• Contexts Γ containing only ⊢+ declarations
• Judgments of the form Γ ⊢+ J
• Derivations using only rules that produce ⊢+ conclusions from
⊢+ premises
Proposition 8.2 (Coherent Fragment and HoTT). The coherent frag-
ment OHTT+ is a weakening of HoTT: every OHTT+ derivation is
valid, but OHTT+ does not include the totality principles (transport, Kan
filling) that HoTT provides. Adding those principles recovers HoTT.
Thus: HoTT is exactly the part of OHTT that never mentions gaps,
augmented with totality.
8.2
What HoTT Cannot Say
We enumerate the expressive limitations of HoTT that OHTT over-
comes.
Proposition 8.3 (No Witnessed Non-Identity). In HoTT, there is no
judgment form for “x and y are witnessed as non-identical.” The closest
approximation is:
¬(x =A y) := (x =A y) →0
which says: “any path from x to y leads to absurdity.”
This is weaker than witnessed non-identity:
• ¬(x =A y) is a function type—to inhabit it, we must construct a
function
• Γ ⊢−(p : x =A y) is a direct witness—positive evidence of
non-coherence
Proposition 8.4 (No Transport Failure). In HoTT, given t : B(x) and
p : x =A y, the term transport(p, t) : B(y) is always well-defined. There
is no way to express “transport fails.”
In OHTT, we can have Γ ⊢−(transport(p, t) : B(y))—witnessed
transport failure.

8.3. THE KAN CONDITION
Proposition 8.5 (No Obstruction Data). In HoTT, obstructions (mon-

as ordinary types or terms. There is no internal representation of “this horn
is unfillable.”
In OHTT, gap witnesses are obstruction data—first-class terms
recording why coherence fails.
8.3
The Kan Condition
The technical heart of the difference is the Kan condition.
Definition 8.6 (Kan Condition (Semantic)). A simplicial set X satis-
fies the Kan condition if every horn has a filler:
∀n ≥1, ∀0 ≤k ≤n, ∀h : Λn
k →X, ∃σ : ∆n →X extending h
Theorem 8.7 (HoTT Models are Kan). In the simplicial model of HoTT,
every type is interpreted as a Kan complex. The Kan condition is what
makes:
• Path inversion work (every path has an inverse)
• Path composition work (paths compose)
• Transport work (terms move along paths)
• Higher coherences work (homotopies exist when needed)
Theorem 8.8 (OHTT Models are Ruptured Kan). In the simplicial
model of OHTT, types are interpreted as ruptured Kan complexes. The
Kan condition is replaced by the trichotomy: every horn is coherently filled,
gap-witnessed, or open.
The Kan condition is the totality assumption that HoTT makes.
OHTT drops this assumption.
8.4
Conservativity
Theorem 8.9 (OHTT is Conservative over HoTT). If J is a judgment
expressible in HoTT and Γ ⊢+ J is derivable in OHTT (where Γ is a coherent
context), then Γ ⊢J is derivable in HoTT.

Proof sketch. OHTT adds gap judgments and horn types, but does
not change the rules for deriving coherence judgments. A derivation
of Γ ⊢+ J in OHTT that uses only coherence rules is exactly a HoTT
derivation.
Conservativity means: OHTT does not prove new coherence facts
about HoTT-expressible judgments. What it adds is the ability to
express and prove gap facts.

The Univalence Axiom
Univalence is the characteristic axiom of HoTT:
ua : (A ≃B) →(A =U B)
Together with function extensionality, it implies that equivalences
are identities (in the universe).

OHTT as an axiom about coherence:
⊢+ ua : (A ≃B) →(A =U B)
This asserts that every equivalence coherently induces a path.
With univalence, OHTT includes HoTT as its coherent fragment.
Without univalence, OHTT includes MLTT.
Remark 8.11. Univalence does not eliminate gaps. Even with univa-

• Gapped paths: ⊢−(p : x =A y) for non-equivalent x, y
• Gapped transport: ⊢−(transport(p, t) : B(y)) when transport is
obstructed
• Gapped equivalences: ⊢−(e : A ≃B) for non-equivalent types
Univalence tells us that equivalences give paths; it does not tell us
which paths or equivalences exist.

— The distinction
We now situate OHTT relative to logical traditions that have grap-
pled with negation, contradiction, and non-classical truth values. The
gap judgment ⊢−is none of the standard notions of negation—but it
has relatives in the literature that illuminate its nature.
9.1
Classical Negation
Definition 9.1 (Classical Negation). In classical logic, negation ¬P is
characterized by:
• Excluded middle: P ∨¬P is a theorem
• Double negation elimination: ¬¬P →P is a theorem
• Semantic: ¬P is true iff P is false
Classical negation is the complement of truth: ¬P holds exactly
when P fails.
Proposition 9.2 (Gap is not Classical Negation). In OHTT:
1. There is no excluded middle: a judgment may be neither coherent nor
gapped (it may be open)
2. There is no double negation elimination: ⊢−⊢−J (a gap-of-gap, if it
made sense) does not give ⊢+ J

3. Gap is not the complement of coherence: open judgments are neither
The trichotomy (coherent / gapped / open) is fundamentally
incompatible with classical two-valued logic.
9.2
Intuitionistic Negation
Definition 9.3 (Intuitionistic Negation). In intuitionistic logic, nega-
tion is defined:
¬P := P →⊥
A proof of ¬P is a function that transforms any proof of P into a
proof of absurdity.
Intuitionistic negation is constructive: to prove ¬P, we must show
that P leads to contradiction. But it is also derivative: negation is
defined in terms of implication and absurdity.
Proposition 9.4 (Gap is not Intuitionistic Negation). In OHTT:
1. Gap is primitive, not defined via implication
2. Γ ⊢−J does not mean “Γ ⊢+ J implies absurdity”
3. A gap witness is direct evidence, not a refutation function
The difference is subtle but crucial:
• Intuitionistic ¬P: “Show me a proof of P, and I’ll derive a con-
tradiction”
• OHTT ⊢−J: “I have witnessed that J does not cohere” (no
hypothetical, no derivation)

The closest intuitionistic relative is Nelson’s strong negation.
Definition 9.5 (Strong Negation). In Nelson’s constructive logic with
strong negation, there are two negations:
• Weak negation: ¬P := P →⊥(standard intuitionistic)
• Strong negation: ∼P (primitive, with its own rules)

Epilogue: What Remains
Open
A theory is not a tomb.

— On incompleteness
We have presented Open Horn Type Theory as a self-contained
formal system: the calculus (Part I), the geometric model (Part II),
the worked obstructions (Part III), and the contrasts with existing
logics (Part IV). But a theory worth its name opens more questions
than it closes. We conclude by indicating what remains.
Computational Content
OHTT as presented is a logic, not a programming language. The co-
herent fragment (HoTT/MLTT) has well-understood computational
content: terms compute, types classify programs, the correspondence
with λ-calculus is tight.
What is the computational content of gaps?
One direction: gap witnesses as certificates. A gap witness ω :
Γ ⊢−J could be a data structure that certifies why J fails—a coun-
terexample, a resource-insufficiency proof, an obstruction cocycle.
This connects to certified programming, proof-carrying code, and
SMT solvers.
Another direction: gap witnesses as exceptions or effects. Transport
failure could raise an exception; the gap witness is the exception
payload. This connects to effect systems, monadic programming,
and algebraic effects.
A third direction: gap witnesses as constraints. In constraint-
based type inference, unification may fail with a witness explaining
why. OHTT could model such systems with gaps as failed unification
witnesses.
95

CHAPTER 9. NEGATION AND PARACONSISTENCY
None of these is developed here. The computational interpreta-
tion of OHTT is open.
Dynamics and Time
OHTT as presented is static: contexts, judgments, and witnesses
are given timelessly. But the motivating examples—texts evolving,
meanings drifting, derivability changing with resources—are inher-
ently dynamic.
A dynamic OHTT would have:
• Time-indexed contexts: Γt at time t
• Judgment evolution: Γt ⊢+ J may become Γt′ ⊢−J or vice versa
• Persistent horns: horns that survive across time, tracking stable
tensions
• A scheduler: an agent that decides which judgments to witness,
when
The scheduler is particularly important. In the static theory, the
trichotomy (coherent/gapped/open) is a fact. In the dynamic theory,
it is a choice: which horns to fill, which to leave open, which to witness
as gapped. A healthy scheduler neither compulsively closes all gaps
nor refuses all coherence. The dynamics of witnessing—when to
press for closure, when to dwell in openness—is not part of the
calculus. It is a separate theory, building on OHTT but not reducible
to it.
This is open.
Empirical Instantiation
The semantic examples of Chapter 6 gesture at a connection to empir-
ical linguistics and natural language processing. But the connection
is not made precise.
An empirical instantiation would:
• Define L (lexical space) and M (meaning space) concretely—perhaps
as embedding spaces from neural language models

• Define coherence and gap in terms of geometric structure—perhaps
via persistent homology, ˇCech complexes, or Vietoris-Rips filtra-

• Extract horns from data—identify where lexical paths fail to lift
to meaning paths
• Validate against linguistic judgments—do the computed gaps

This would connect OHTT to topological data analysis, distri-
butional semantics, and computational linguistics. The tools exist
(persistent homology, word embeddings, attention patterns). The
bridge is not built.
This is open.
Higher Gaps
OHTT has gaps at all levels: gaps between terms, gaps between
paths, gaps between homotopies. The higher structure is sketched
but not fully developed.
Questions:
• What is the homotopy theory of ruptured Kan complexes? Is
there a model structure?
• What are the higher obstruction invariants? Characteristic classes
for ruptured fibrations?
• Is there a ruptured ∞-topos? What are its internal logic and type
theory?
• How does truncation interact with gaps? Is there a ruptured
n-type theory?
The foundations are laid. The higher theory is open.
Categorical Semantics
HoTT has multiple categorical semantics: (∞, 1)-toposes, model
categories, type-theoretic fibration categories. OHTT should have
analogous semantics, but they are not developed here.

Implementations
HoTT has proof assistants: Agda with cubical mode, Coq with HoTT

An OHTT proof assistant would need:
• Syntax for ⊢+ and ⊢−judgments
• Type-checking that respects Exclusion
• Horn types as first-class constructions
• Perhaps: tactics for constructing gap witnesses
The implementation is open.
Beyond
Finally, there are directions that extend beyond the formal:
• The ethics of witnessing: When should we witness a gap rather
than leave it open? When should we seek coherence rather than
rest in rupture? These are not formal questions, but they arise
from the formalism.
